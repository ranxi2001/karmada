One clarification about the code that appears to hard-code `member1` as the target.

[`targetClusterNames`](https://github.com/karmada-io/karmada/blob/3d4d14d746de507164abf40c1017b1f2b0e47e3a/test/e2e/suites/base/tainttoleration_test.go#L140-L146) is an observation, not policy configuration. It reads the current `ResourceBinding` and checks that its only target equals [`framework.ClusterNames()[0]`](https://github.com/karmada-io/karmada/blob/3d4d14d746de507164abf40c1017b1f2b0e47e3a/test/e2e/suites/base/tainttoleration_test.go#L47-L64). The policy candidate set is still [all `framework.ClusterNames()`](https://github.com/karmada-io/karmada/blob/3d4d14d746de507164abf40c1017b1f2b0e47e3a/test/e2e/suites/base/tainttoleration_test.go#L66-L77). The initial result is `{member1:3}` because only member1's taint is tolerated, not because the policy pins the binding to member1.

The taint fixture itself is a [3-replica Deployment requesting `10m` CPU per replica](https://github.com/karmada-io/karmada/blob/3d4d14d746de507164abf40c1017b1f2b0e47e3a/test/helper/resource.go#L190-L218). After the assertion passes, [`AfterEach` removes all taints before `DeferCleanup` deletes the objects](https://github.com/karmada-io/karmada/blob/3d4d14d746de507164abf40c1017b1f2b0e47e3a/test/e2e/suites/base/tainttoleration_test.go#L104-L137). In the [official job](https://github.com/karmada-io/karmada/actions/runs/28998390044/job/86054168911), that binding was requeued at `07:10:24.146`, became feasible on all three clusters at `.170`, was assigned `{member1:3, member2:3, member3:3}` at `.195`, and was patched at `.239`. The scheduler then [records assumptions for every target](https://github.com/karmada-io/karmada/blob/3d4d14d746de507164abf40c1017b1f2b0e47e3a/pkg/scheduler/scheduler.go#L683-L775): `30m` per cluster, or 9 assumed replicas / `90m` across three clusters.

This does not mean that the Deployment ran on member2/member3. The affected Flink test [targets only member1, adds `200m` per FlinkDeployment, and stops at the first no-fit](https://github.com/karmada-io/karmada/blob/3d4d14d746de507164abf40c1017b1f2b0e47e3a/test/e2e/suites/base/estimator_test.go#L379-L515), so the relevant foreign reservation is member1's `30m`, not the three-cluster `90m`. It did not need to exhaust member1 by itself; it only had to push one `200m` attempt over the boundary. That `30m` was present at the no-fit at `07:15:22.998`; after it disappeared, the `200m` probe fit at `07:15:24.668`. Cleanup therefore made the consumer stop at a boundary its own workloads had not reached alone.
